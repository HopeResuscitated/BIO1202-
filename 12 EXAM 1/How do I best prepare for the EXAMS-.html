How do I best prepare for the EXAMS?

When students ask me the best way to prepare for the exam, this is the approach that I suggest:   

  1. My notes (the ones we fill in during lecture) should be your primary tool to learn the materials.  When I create my lecture PowerPoints, I take the MOST important materials out of the textbook chapters.  I am assuming that you have read each chapter fully.  Re-reading the chapters again and again is not the best use of your time.  If there is a topic you are not understanding, then going back and re-reading that area is recommended, but not entire chapters since my lecture notes are designed to condense and organize that chapter materials.
  2. I post REVIEW MATERIALS to MOODLE to help you prepare for the exam.  This would include the REVIEW PICTURE FILE.  This file takes the most important pictures/figures from each chapter and these are what I would use to create an exam question.  For each figure, I typically include some type of voice clip or text clip as to what you should be thinking about when looking at the picture or how I would use this figure for an exam question.  Remember, one picture can convey a LOT of information and could represent a multi-point question.  About 1/2 of the overall points on the exam come from picture-based questions, so if you learn the pictures from the REVIEW PICTURE FILE you already have half of the exam in mastered.  Students have told me that they like to take the REVIEW PICTURE FILE, make a second copy, strip off the labels or identifies, and then see if they can predict what those labels/identifies should be when they are studying.  If they can do that, that is a good predictor that they understand what that figure is trying to convey.
  3. I usually post a REVIEW QUESTION SET with answers to MOODLE.  The BEST way that you should use that file is to ONLY look at it once you are TOTALLY done studying and use it like a mock exam.  No notes, no book, no help.  Take it like an exam and see how well you do.  If you do well, that is usually a good indicator that you understand the materials.
  4. Mastering Biology has a MASSIVE volume of review materials to help you prepare for the exam.  If you take a look at the video I created, I walk you through how to access this material.  The address for this video is below:

    https://lsu.hosted.panopto.com/Panopto/Pages/Viewer.aspx?id=bed0c197-13fd-4ced-b424-aced018891ab

    Again, using the pre-tests and post-tests that MB can create for you are best suited if you save them for when you are totally done studying and treat them like real exams to truly measure how well you understand the materials.  MB has so much helpful materials including vocabulary review, word matching, flash cards, etc.
  5. Mastering Biology HW and QZ assignments.  I would NOT recommend going back and redoing these as review for the exam.  The reason that I say this is that many of these questions are quite long and way more involved than you would see on our exam.  Yes, they are great questions to test your understanding of the materials, but they are not the BEST use of your time.  If you have a lot of EXTRA time, then yes, you can use these assignments as review, but I would focus my efforts of the other items I mentioned.
  6. We also have a Biology and Chemistry Tutorial Center that is operated through the Center for Academic Success.  They offer free tutoring on a walk-in basis done by students who have taken this course before.  They also offer workshops on how to develop effective study strategies, earn better grades, learning tips, etc.  For more information, check out below:

    https://www.lsu.edu/cas/about/services/index.php

  7. And finally...organization is the key to your success.  Doing a little bit of work each week and keep up with the materials will help you stay organized.  If you have not yet done so, make sure you take advantage of ANY and EVERY possible bonus activity.  These would include any type of MOODLE survey that I may post for a chapter.  In some (not all) chapters I may have an activity called a "LECTURE ACTIVITY".  These are not graded but are typically used for bonus on the exam.  You must follow the directions completely, but in most instances, these are short question sets that expand on chapter topics and will help you learn the material at the same time.
Hope that helps!

Below is feedback from students on how they personally prepared for exams:

ANONYMOUS: For studying, I mainly used the resources you provided on Moodle, with the review guide and the PowerPoint slides with the pictures as my main reference. My approach was to go over the review guide and make sure I felt comfortable formulating an answer or had a solid understanding of the information it covered. If I wasn’t completely comfortable with a topic, I referred back to the lecture slides. I found that 2 days was sufficient to review all the material thoroughly. My biggest tip is to not memorize the details by passively looking at the lecture slides and review, but rather be able to explain key concepts in my own words.

ANONYMOUS: I prepared by making quizlets  of your notes for each chapter. I used the notes to study and the picture file. I started to study two weeks before the exam!

ANONYMOUS: When preparing for the exam, I would always use active recall study methods (quizlet, flashcards, etc). This way, I could be sure that I was actually remembering all the information I was learning. The main study material I used was the in-class notes. I started studying these about 3 weeks before the exam. 2 days before the exam, I would highlight where to find the answers for the review outline in my notes, and I would study the review picture file. Additionally, in chapters with lots of classifications/terms that relate to each other, I would create flowcharts to better my understanding of the material.

ANONYMOUS: When studying for this test, I focused most of my studying on the lecture notes. I made sure I understood the concepts by talking through the concepts in my own words. I would also try to make little memorization tips when I couldn't remember something. As I got closer to my exam day, I would look over the review picture file and make sure I understood every picture and what was important about that picture. I did the review sheet when I felt I knew all the material and acted like it was a test. The day before the exam, I did the review questions to have another practice test to see what I'm having trouble with and need to go over. I started studying five days before the exam, and it felt like a sufficient amount of time.
